# About This Sample

This document is an illustrative sample dissertation supplied with Ecrivain so that doctoral students can see how a complete empirical economics dissertation is put together. It is not a real dissertation, it has not been examined, and it must not be submitted, adapted for submission or cited as research.

Kestrel Bay University is a fictional institution. The author, Mateo J. Alvarado, and the committee members, Prof. Lena Hartmann, Prof. Victor Chen and Prof. Amara Nwosu, are fictional, as are all other people, places and organizations named in the acknowledgements and elsewhere. The labor-market panel at the heart of the empirical chapters is synthetic: 600 labor markets modelled on US commuting zones, observed quarterly from 2017 to 2024, generated for illustration. None of its numbers describes any real place, and no estimate in this document should be read as a finding about the United States or any other economy.

The literature, by contrast, is real. Every work cited appears in the reading list that accompanies the sample, and it is characterized only in general terms; there are no quotations from it and no invented page numbers.

The sample demonstrates the conventions of a dissertation in applied economics: a motivating introduction with explicit research questions, a literature review organized by argument, a formal spatial-equilibrium model with numbered propositions and display equations, an empirical strategy built on modern staggered difference-in-differences estimators, regression tables with standard errors, event-study charts, robustness appendices and proofs. Citations are formatted in Chicago author-date style.
