# Abstract

AI coding assistants built on large language models are now routine features of professional development environments, yet most evidence about their effects comes from controlled tasks or individual-level telemetry rather than from teams working on production systems. This thesis reports a convergent mixed-methods, embedded multiple-case study of how three teams at Lanternfish Systems, a business-to-business software company of 140 engineers, adopted an AI coding assistant rolled out on March 3, 2025. The teams differed in stack and stakes: Atlas (payments and payroll services, 16 engineers), Beacon (customer web front end, 15 engineers) and Cormorant (data platform and reporting, 13 engineers).

The quantitative strand mined pull requests for 26 weeks before and 26 weeks after adoption and fitted an interrupted time series model to each team. The qualitative strand combined a survey (38 of 44 engineers, 86%) with 18 semi-structured interviews analyzed through reflexive thematic analysis. The strands were integrated in a joint display.

Beacon showed a clear level increase of 0.46 merged pull requests per engineer-week (95% CI 0.21 to 0.71) and a shorter median cycle time. Cormorant's delivery indicators barely moved. Atlas showed a smaller, uncertain throughput gain, larger pull requests, heavier review and a rise in static-analysis security findings that reversed after the team adopted explicit assistant norms in week 12. Engineers described acceleration concentrated in boilerplate and tests, a shift of verification effort from authors to reviewers, trust calibrated to the cost of error, uneven learning effects by seniority, and team agreements as the decisive factor.

The thesis argues that the assistant's effect was a property of the team rather than of the tool. It proposes a team-level adoption model in which task verifiability, stakes and explicit norms jointly shape outcomes, maps the model onto the SPACE dimensions of productivity, and offers governance recommendations for engineering organizations.

**Keywords:** AI coding assistants, large language models, developer productivity, code review, software security, mixed methods, case study
