# Appendix B: Interview and Focus Group Guides

This appendix reproduces the topic guides used in the qualitative component of the feasibility study reported in Chapter 5 (Paper 3). The study comprised 22 semi-structured interviews and 3 focus groups with construction workers and supervisors. The guides were semi-structured: the questions set the ground to be covered, but interviewers followed participants' lead and used prompts only where a topic had not arisen naturally. They are reported here to support transparency in line with COREQ {{tong2007}}, and their structure followed the process evaluation domains of implementation, mechanisms and context {{moore2015}}.

Interviews lasted between about 30 and 50 minutes and took place in a site office or a nearby café, away from the work area and without supervisors present for worker interviews. Focus groups were held at the end of a shift. All sessions were audio-recorded with consent.

## B.1 Individual interview guide: workers

**Opening**

1. Could you tell me about your work on this site: what you do, and how long you have done this kind of work?
2. What is a hot day on this job like for you?

**Experience of heat**

3. How do you know when the heat is starting to affect you, or someone else on the crew?
4. What did you usually do on hot days before this summer? *Prompts:* breaks, water, shade, changing pace.

**The programme** (intervention crews only)

5. Over the last few weeks, what did the heat programme mean for your working day?
6. Talk me through the scheduled breaks. When did you take them, and when did you not? *Prompts:* what made it easier or harder; who decided.
7. How did you find the shaded rest area and the water stations?
8. What was it like having a buddy check on you, and checking on someone else?

**Pay, pace and the crew**

9. How does the way you are paid affect whether you stop in the heat? *Prompt:* days paid by output compared with days paid by the hour.
10. How do others on the crew react when someone takes a break in the heat?
11. What part does your supervisor play in whether people rest?

**Closing**

12. If this programme carried on, what would you keep, change or drop?
13. Is there anything about working in the heat that I have not asked about?

## B.2 Individual interview guide: supervisors

1. Could you describe your role and your crew?
2. How did you manage hot days before this summer?
3. What did the supervisor training cover, in your words, and what was useful or not useful about it?
4. How did you decide when to call a break? *Prompts:* the WBGT reading, deadlines, the crew's mood.
5. What pressures from above or from the schedule affected the work–rest plan?
6. How did the crew respond to scheduled breaks and buddy checks?
7. What would need to change for a programme like this to work on every site?

## B.3 Focus group guide (mixed crew groups)

The focus groups aimed to explore shared norms and points of disagreement within crews, rather than to collect individual accounts. The facilitator, a member of the research team, opened by explaining that there were no right answers, that disagreement was welcome and that nothing said would be reported to the employer in a way that identified anyone.

1. **Warm-up.** Think back to the hottest day of the last few weeks. What happened on site?
2. **Group norms.** On this crew, what does it say about someone if they stop to rest in the heat?
3. **The programme.** Which parts of the heat programme worked for this crew, and which did not? *Activity:* participants placed cards for each component (work–rest schedule, shade, water, buddy checks, supervisor training) on a line from "worked well" to "did not work".
4. **Trade-offs.** When the schedule says rest but the job says keep going, what usually happens, and why?
5. **The future.** If the company asked this group to design next summer's heat plan, what would be in it?

## B.4 Changes to the guides during fieldwork

After the first six interviews, question 9 was moved earlier in the worker guide, because pay arose spontaneously in most early interviews and participants found it awkward to return to later. A prompt about buddy checks feeling like being watched was added after it was raised in the first focus group. Analysis followed the thematic approach of {{braun2006|n}}, and sample adequacy was judged by information power rather than saturation {{malterud2016}}.
