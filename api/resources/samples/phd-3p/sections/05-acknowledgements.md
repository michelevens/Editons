# Acknowledgements

My first thanks go to the construction workers, supervisors and site managers who let me stand beside them through a hot summer, answered my questions at the end of long shifts, and tolerated a researcher carrying a heat-stress monitor and a box of sample cups. I hope this thesis repays a little of their patience.

I am deeply grateful to my supervisors. Professor Elias Mwangi believed the project could be done when it was still a page of questions, opened doors that I could not have opened alone, and taught me that a good study design is a moral as well as a technical achievement. Dr Clara Benedetti read every draft with a precision I came to rely on, and her questions about what my estimates actually meant improved each paper.

Dr Joseph Otieno gave his time and expertise to the worksite temperature estimates in Paper 2, and Dr Leila Haddad made the fieldwork in Paper 3 possible through her standing with the crews and her practical sense of what a site will and will not accept. The Coastal Region Workers' Compensation Board provided de-identified claims data, and I thank its data team for answering many questions about coding practice.

This research was supported by a Kestrel Bay University Doctoral Scholarship and a School of Public Health fieldwork grant. The funders had no role in the design, analysis or reporting of the studies.

Colleagues in the doctoral room on the fourth floor made the years shorter and the coffee stronger. My parents, who worked outdoors for much of their lives, are the reason I asked these questions at all. And to Arjun, who listened to every version of every argument, thank you.
