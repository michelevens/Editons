# Acknowledgements

My first debt is to Dr. Helen Varga, who supervised this thesis with patience and a sharp pencil, and who asked, early and often, what the numbers in a loan register could and could not tell me. Dr. Samuel Okoro read the full draft as second reader and pushed me to think harder about the federal charter and about what the archive leaves out.

The staff of the Kestrel Bay Historical Society made the records of the Kestrel Bay Teachers' Credit Union available to me over two long summers, retrieved boxes I had no right to expect on short notice, and shared their own knowledge of the collection. I am grateful to the volunteers of the Kestrel Bay Oral History Project, and above all to Ruth Ellison, who spent an afternoon telling me what she remembered of the Tuesday and Friday office off the Lakeside High School library.

Fellow students in the department's graduate workshop read early chapters and improved them. The Department of History provided a small research grant that paid for photocopying and transcription.

Finally, thank you to my family, who heard more about salary warrants than anyone should.
