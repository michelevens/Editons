# About This Sample

This thesis is an illustrative sample supplied with Ecrivain to show students what a complete master's thesis in history looks like: its front matter, chapter structure, footnotes, tables, figures and bibliography. It is not a real piece of research and must not be submitted, adapted for submission or cited as evidence of anything.

Kestrel Bay University, the city of Kestrel Bay, the Kestrel Bay Teachers' Credit Union, Kestrel Bay Savings & Trust, the Kestrel Bay Historical Society and its manuscript collection MS 214, the *Kestrel Bay Evening Courier*, the Kestrel Bay Oral History Project and every person named in the text, including the author, supervisor, readers, union officers, members and the oral-history narrator, are fictional. The archival documents, newspaper reports, interview testimony, membership figures, loan records and other data are synthetic, invented to illustrate how a historian reports and interprets such material. Any resemblance to real people or institutions is unintended.

The national events and laws the thesis mentions, such as the banking panics of the early 1930s, the bank holiday of March 1933, the Banking Act of 1933 and the Federal Credit Union Act of 1934, are real. So are the published works cited in the footnotes and listed in the bibliography. They are paraphrased, never quoted, and are characterized only in general terms.

The sample uses Chicago notes and bibliography style. Citations appear as numbered footnotes, given in full at first mention and shortened afterwards, and are collected in a bibliography at the end. Discursive footnotes carry the references to the fictional archive, newspaper and oral history, showing how a history thesis documents unpublished sources alongside published scholarship.
