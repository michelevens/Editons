# Acknowledgements

My first thanks go to the fourteen students at Riverside State University who gave me an hour or more of time they did not have, often late at night, after a shift, or with a child asleep in the next room. You told me about your programs with honesty and humor, and nine of you wrote back after reading my summaries to tell me what I had understood and what I had not. I hope this thesis does justice to what you shared.

I am grateful to my supervisor, Dr. Miriam Achterberg, who read every draft with care, asked the questions I was avoiding, and kept reminding me that a theme is an argument and not a box. Her patience with my reflexive journal, and her insistence that I write myself into the methodology rather than around it, made this a better piece of work.

Thanks also to the program coordinators at Riverside State University who circulated my invitation in their newsletters, and to the members of the Kestrel Bay graduate writing group, whose Thursday sessions kept me moving through the winter.

Finally, to my mother, who never went to college and who still asks me to explain what a thesis is: this one is for you, and for all of us.
