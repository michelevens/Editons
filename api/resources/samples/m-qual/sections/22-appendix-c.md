# Appendix C: Codebook Excerpt

This appendix shows how a small number of initial codes were grouped into subthemes and themes. In reflexive thematic analysis, a codebook of this kind is an audit record of interpretive decisions, not a coding frame to be applied consistently by several coders; codes were developed and revised throughout analysis, and no reliability statistics were calculated {{braun2019;braun2022}}. Of the 212 initial codes generated, one or two per subtheme are shown here. Labels are as they stood at the end of phase two (coding); definitions were written during phase five.

### Illustrative coding note

The following entry from the reflexive journal, written during phase four (developing and reviewing themes), illustrates how codes were reconsidered:

> Thinking again about "group chat answers first." I had it sitting under a candidate theme about help-seeking, but read across the dataset it is doing more than that. The chat is where the rules get decoded, where people feel safe, and also where some people are shut out. It needs to be its own theme, and the exclusion extracts need to stay with it rather than being moved somewhere tidier.

This note marks the point at which "We built our own room" became a separate theme, with subtheme 3c retained to keep the exclusionary side of peer networks in view, as discussed in Chapter 5.
