# Acknowledgements

My first debt is to my supervisors. Professor Gideon Marsh read every draft as if it mattered, which is the best thing a supervisor can do, and the thesis of this dissertation is partly a description of what I learned by watching him. Dr Ines Albuquerque pressed me again and again on the law, saving me from several confident mistakes about fiduciary doctrine and from at least one about autonomy.

Colleagues in the Department of Philosophy at Kestrel Bay University heard rough versions of these chapters in the Thursday work-in-progress seminar and were kinder than the material deserved. I thank in particular Tomasz Wierzbicki, whose objection about demandingness reshaped Chapter 6, and Hana Okafor, who insisted that the frequent attender in Chapter 7 deserved a fuller hearing than I had first given her.

A doctoral studentship from the Faculty of Arts and Humanities gave me three years in which to think, and the library staff found books I had given up on.

My parents taught me, without ever saying so, what it is to be listened to properly. Rafe kept the household going through the final year and never once asked when I would be finished. This dissertation is for them.
