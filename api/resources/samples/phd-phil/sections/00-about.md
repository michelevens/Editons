# About This Sample

This dissertation is an illustrative sample supplied with Ecrivain so that students can see what a complete doctoral dissertation in philosophy looks like from title page to Works Cited. It is not a real dissertation and must not be submitted, in whole or in part, for any degree or assessment.

Kestrel Bay University is fictional, and so is everyone named in the front matter: the author, Eleanor S. Whitcombe, her supervisors, Professor Gideon Marsh and Dr Ines Albuquerque, and the colleagues thanked in the Acknowledgements. The advisers and clients in the opening vignette and in the three cases of Chapter 7 were made up as thought experiments. They describe no real person, firm, surgery or university.

The works cited are real, and the dissertation describes them only in general terms. It paraphrases rather than quotes and gives no page numbers. Students who use a work listed here should read it for themselves before relying on any characterisation of it.

The sample shows the conventions of a humanities dissertation: an argument built from numbered definitions and replies to objections, chapters that signpost what comes before and after, citations in MLA 9th edition style, discursive footnotes for qualifications and asides, and a generated Works Cited list. No empirical data appear anywhere in it. The argument is philosophical throughout, and its cases are invented to test concepts, not to report findings.
