# Abstract

**Background:** Inpatient falls are a frequent and costly source of preventable harm. Purposeful hourly rounding is widely adopted to prevent them, but evidence for its effect is mixed and depends on fidelity.

**Local problem:** On 5 West, a 32-bed adult medical-surgical unit in a community teaching hospital, the fall rate during the baseline year (July 2023–June 2024) was 4.53 falls per 1,000 patient-days, above the hospital's target of 3.0. Most baseline falls occurred overnight, and rounding was informal and undocumented.

**Methods:** The project used the Model for Improvement and is reported according to SQUIRE 2.0. The outcome measure was the monthly fall rate, displayed on a run chart against the baseline median of 4.71. The process measure was the percentage of 40 randomly audited patient-hours per month with rounding documented. Balancing measures were call-light use, rounding time, staff-perceived workload, patient-reported responsiveness and pressure injuries.

**Interventions:** Registered nurses and nursing assistants delivered alternating purposeful rounds, hourly by day and two-hourly overnight, using a "4 Ps" script (pain, position, potty, possessions) with an alarm and environment check. Three Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles (July 2024–March 2025) moved from a one-hallway pilot to unit-wide rounding with proactive toileting for high-risk patients, then to electronic documentation, charge nurse huddles and post-fall huddles.

**Results:** During the nine-month intervention period the fall rate was 2.62 per 1,000 patient-days (20 falls in 7,634 patient-days), a 42% relative reduction that met the aim. Seven consecutive points from September 2024 to March 2025 fell below the baseline median, indicating a shift. Injurious falls declined from 0.89 to 0.39 per 1,000 patient-days. Documented rounding rose from 58% to 90%, averaging 87.3% from October to March. Call-light use decreased, and perceived workload did not increase.

**Conclusions:** On this unit, structured rounding coincided with a sustained, non-random reduction in falls. The uncontrolled single-unit design limits causal inference, and sustaining the gains will depend on leadership attention and embedded documentation.

*Keywords:* inpatient falls, purposeful hourly rounding, quality improvement, PDSA, run chart, medical-surgical nursing
