# Appendix B: Usability Test Script and SUS Questionnaire

This appendix reproduces the materials of the usability study in Chapter 6. The script was read in English or Kiswahili as the participant preferred.

## B.1 Facilitator script

**Welcome (about 5 minutes).** Thank you for coming. I am Mateo, a student at Kestrel Bay University. Today we are testing a phone app called Dose, which helps community health workers keep track of follow-up visits. We are testing the app, not you. Taking part has nothing to do with your work assessment, and you can stop at any time. All the patients in the app are invented. With your permission, we will record your voice and the phone screen; only the research team will use the recordings. [Obtain written consent.]

**Introduction to the app (about 2 minutes).** This is the phone you will use. To open Dose, tap this icon and enter the PIN 1-2-3-4. That is all I will show you. I would like you to discover the rest as you would if a colleague had just shared the app with you.

**Think-aloud instructions.** While you work, please say out loud what you are looking at and trying to do. If you go quiet, I may remind you. I cannot help during the tasks, because we want to see where the app is unclear; if you are stuck, tell me you want to move on.

**Tasks (about 25 minutes).** I will give you five task cards, one at a time. Please read each card, tell me when you start, and tell me when you think you have finished.

**After the tasks (about 10 minutes).** Please complete this short questionnaire about the app. There are no right or wrong answers; tell us what you really think. [Administer SUS.] Then: What did you find most useful? What was most difficult? Is there anything you would change before you used this in your work?

**Close.** Thank you. [Provide transport allowance and confirm the participant's code.]

## B.2 Task cards

Each card was printed with the task and any information the participant needed. The phone started in airplane mode.

- **T1. Find overdue patients.** "It is the start of your working day. Find out which of your patients are overdue for a follow-up visit. Tell me how many there are."
- **T2. Record a visit offline.** "You have just visited Amina Wekesa. She was at home and is taking her medicine as prescribed. Record this visit." (Visit outcome slip provided.)
- **T3. Reschedule a follow-up.** "The clinic has asked you to see Daniel Otieno again on the date written on this slip, earlier than planned. Change his next follow-up date."
- **T4. Sync after reconnecting.** [Facilitator switches on the hotspot and turns off airplane mode.] "You are now back in an area with network. Make sure today's records reach the district server."
- **T5. Switch language.** "Change the app into your other language, and then change it back."

*Note.* Patient names on the task cards were invented for the study and do not refer to real people.

## B.3 Observation sheet

For each task, the observer recorded start and end time, completion (assisted attempts counted as failures), taps where observable, errors and recoveries, notable remarks, and any usability problem with a provisional severity rating, reviewed against the recordings after each day.

## B.4 System Usability Scale

The System Usability Scale was administered in its standard ten-item form {{brooke2013}}, with "system" replaced by "app" in the participant-facing version. Each item was rated on a five-point scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The Kiswahili version was translated for the study and back-translated by a second speaker.

1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the system was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
9. I felt very confident using the system.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

**Scoring.** For odd-numbered items, the contribution is the scale position minus 1. For even-numbered items, the contribution is 5 minus the scale position. The ten contributions are summed and multiplied by 2.5, so that SUS = 2.5 × Σ(adjusted item scores), giving a score from 0 to 100 {{brooke2013}}. Scores were interpreted against published benchmarks {{bangor2008;sauro2016}}, as reported in Section 6.3.3.
