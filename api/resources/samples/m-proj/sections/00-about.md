# About This Sample

This document is an illustrative sample thesis supplied with Ecrivain. It shows how a complete, project-based master's thesis in human-computer interaction can be organized, from the abstract through requirements, design, implementation and evaluation to a closing reflection. It is meant to be read as a model of structure, signposting and academic register, and as a starting point for the templates the app offers. It is not a thesis that anyone has submitted, and it must not be submitted, adapted for submission, or cited as research.

Everything specific in it is invented. Kestrel Bay University, the School of Computing, the author Mateo J. Ferreira, the supervisor Dr. Ingrid Solberg, Lowmoor District, the community health workers and supervisors, and the software artifact called Dose do not exist. All data reported in the chapters, including field observations, interview extracts, task completion rates, timings and System Usability Scale scores, are synthetic. They were created to show how such a study is reported, and no participants were recruited.

The works cited are real publications from a fixed reading list, and the citations are inserted by the app in IEEE style. They are paraphrased rather than quoted, and readers should consult the original sources before relying on any of them. Where the text marks a claim with "[source needed]", the gap is deliberate: it shows students how to flag an argument that still needs support.

Use the sample to see how the parts fit together, how research questions are carried through every chapter, and how a design project accounts for its own decisions.
