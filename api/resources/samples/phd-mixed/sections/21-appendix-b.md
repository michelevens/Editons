# Appendix B: Interview Protocol

This is the semi-structured protocol used in Phase 2 (Section 4.7). Core questions were asked of every participant in roughly this order; probes, shown indented in italics, were used as needed. The protocol was built around the Phase 1 results so that each section could follow a quantitative thread into teachers' accounts (Section 4.8.2). Interviews lasted between 45 and 70 minutes and were conducted by video call.

## Opening (about 5 minutes)

Before recording: thank the participant, confirm consent to audio recording, remind them that they may skip any question or stop at any time, and ask them not to name students. Briefly introduce myself, including my previous work as a secondary science teacher.

1. To start, could you tell me a little about your teaching this year: what you teach, and what a typical week looks like for you?

## Part 1: The dashboard in the week (about 10 minutes)

2. Where, if anywhere, does the district dashboard fit into that week?
   *Probe: Are there particular days, meetings or moments when you open it? Who or what prompts you?*
3. Think about the last time you used the dashboard. Could you walk me through it from start to finish: what you were looking for, what you looked at, and what you did afterwards?
   *Probe: Would you be willing to share your screen and show me what you looked at? (Please keep student names out of view.)*
   *Probe: Was that a typical occasion? What would a typical one look like?*

## Part 2: Trust in the data (about 10 minutes)

4. How far do you feel you can rely on what the dashboard tells you about your students?
   *Probe: Can you think of a time when the data matched what you knew about a student? A time when it did not?*
   *Probe: How current are the data when you see them? Does that matter for what you do?*
   *Probe: What do you make of the at-risk flags?*
5. Has your view of the data changed since you first started using the dashboard? What changed it?

## Part 3: Time and conditions (about 8 minutes)

6. When you think about using the dashboard more, or differently, what gets in the way?
   *Probe: Is there time in your week for this? Where would it come from?*
   *Probe: What support, training or help have you had? Was it the kind of help you needed?*

## Part 4: From seeing to acting (about 10 minutes)

7. When you look at the dashboard and see something, what happens next?
   *Probe: Can you give me an example where it changed what you did in class? An example where it did not?*
   *Probe: What would the dashboard need to show you to make it easier to decide what to do?*
8. Do you ever use the information from the dashboard outside the dashboard itself, for example by exporting it, printing it or sharing it?
   *Probe: How often? What for? Who sees it?*

## Part 5: Purpose and audience (about 8 minutes)

9. Who do you think the dashboard is mainly for?
   *Probe: Do others, such as department leads or administrators, look at the same data? How does that affect how you use it?*
   *Probe: How is the dashboard talked about in your school, for example in PLC meetings or staff meetings?*

## Part 6: Reflecting on the survey (about 7 minutes)

Read the summary that matches the participant's profile; do not name the profile.

- *For all participants:* "In the survey, teachers who trusted the data more were much more likely to turn their plans to use the dashboard into actual use. Does that fit your experience? Why might that be?"
- *Additionally, for participants in the Willing but stalled profile:* "Many teachers said they intended to use the dashboard but reported using it rarely. What do you think explains that?"
- *Additionally, where log data and self-report differed for the participant:* "Some teachers' login counts were quite different from how often they said they used the dashboard. Why do you think that might happen?"

## Closing (about 3 minutes)

10. If you could change one thing about the dashboard, or about how your district uses it, what would it be?
11. Is there anything we have not talked about that you think I should know?

After recording: thank the participant, explain the transcript review option and the timeline for the study, and remind them how to contact the research team. Write a reflective memo within 24 hours.
