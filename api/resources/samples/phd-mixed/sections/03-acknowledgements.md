# Acknowledgements

This dissertation owes its existence to the teachers of the Northern Lakes region, who gave up evenings and planning periods that, as many of them told me, they did not really have. Four hundred and twelve of them answered a long survey, and twenty-four of them talked with me at length about what they do, and do not do, with the district dashboard. I hope they recognize their working lives in these pages.

My chair, Professor Adaeze Nwankwo, read every draft with the same patience and the same sharp pencil. She taught me to ask what a finding is evidence of before asking whether it is significant, and she never let me hide behind a fit index. Dr. Felipe Ortega guided me through the structural models and the latent profile analysis, and more than once talked me out of a model that was clever rather than sensible. Dr. Hannah Weiss, my external examiner, asked the question about time that reshaped the second half of the study.

I thank the research offices of the four districts for their trust and their careful attention to teachers' privacy, and the district data coordinators who explained, with good humor, how the dashboard's nightly updates actually worked.

My doctoral cohort at the Graduate School of Education kept me honest and kept me fed. Particular thanks go to the Thursday writing group, whose members read early chapters that no one should have had to read.

The Kestrel Bay University Graduate Research Fund supported transcription costs and a semester of writing time.

Finally, to Maren, who lived with this project for five years and still asks good questions about it, and to my parents, both teachers, who first showed me that the most important data in a classroom rarely appear on a screen: thank you.
